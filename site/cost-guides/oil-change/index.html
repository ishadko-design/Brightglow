Why quotes run $35 to $175

Oil type
Conventional $35–$55; blend $50–$75; full synthetic $65–$125. The oil is the price — use what the manual calls for, no more.
Oil capacity
Most cars take 5 quarts. Trucks and diesels take 8–10+, at $8–$12 per extra quart. Bigger sump, bigger bill.
Where you go
Quick-lube chains are cheapest; dealers average around $100 and up. European and luxury cars often land $95–$175.
Add-ons
Tire rotation adds $20–$50; premium filters run $15–$25 vs $5–$8 standard. Say no to the upsell menu you didn't ask for.

What the extra money buys

Conventional
$35–$55

Fine for older, simple engines with short intervals. Breaks down faster — you're back in 3,000–5,000 miles.

Full synthetic
$65–$125

What most modern cars spec. Lasts 7,500–15,000 miles and protects better in heat and cold. Costs more per visit, similar per year.

Bottom line: synthetic costs more per visit but lasts 2–3x longer — annual cost comes out roughly even. If the manual says synthetic, that's not optional.

Common questions

How often should I change my oil?

Follow the manual: 5,000–10,000 miles for most modern cars on synthetic. Short city trips and hills — go shorter, not longer.

Can I go longer between changes with synthetic?

Yes — 7,500 to 15,000 miles depending on the car. The oil life monitor isn't guessing; trust it over the windshield sticker.

Is a $30 oil change a good deal?

Check what's in it. Wrong oil, a cheap filter, or an underfilled crankcase turns a bargain into an engine problem.

How long does it take?

30–45 minutes at a shop, including the filter and a basic inspection. DIY saves $25–$60 in parts but takes longer the first time.
